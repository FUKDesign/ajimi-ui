# Research notes for the Ajimi case study

Gathered 27 November 2026 by web research. Only the lines marked **verified** were read on the primary source. Do not publish an unverified figure without checking it first.

## Who is learning Japanese

- **Verified.** 4,000,750 people study Japanese at institutions abroad, across 143 countries and regions, with 80,898 teachers and 19,344 institutions. Learners are up 5.4% on 2021. Japan Foundation, Survey on Japanese-Language Education Abroad 2024. https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/about/press/2025/dl/2025-031-03.pdf
- The count covers institutions only. Self-taught and app learners are outside it, so the real audience for an app is not measured here.
- Unverified: regional shares naming anime, manga, J-pop or fashion as a reason (North America 92.2%, Western Europe 85.5%). No food or travel percentages were found.

## Food as the way in

- **Verified.** Japan had 42,683,600 visitors in 2025 (preliminary), up 15.8% on 2024. JNTO, 21 January 2026. https://www.jnto.go.jp/news/press/20260121_monthly.html
- Unverified: 82.2% of visitors named eating Japanese food as what they most looked forward to (Japan Tourism Agency survey, via Ministry of Finance and Jiji Press). Check the year at https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/ before citing.

## Why cooking should help the words stick

- **Enactment effect.** A meta-analysis of 145 studies found that performing an action phrase is remembered better than reading it. Roberts, MacLeod and Fernandes, 2022. https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/enact22.pdf
- **Testing effect.** Being tested on material beats rereading it after a week's delay. Roediger and Karpicke, 2006, Psychological Science.
- **Context.** The quality of the context a word appears in mattered more than how often it appeared. Webb, 2008. https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item/178
- Task-based language teaching (Ellis 2003, Long 2015) and dual coding (Paivio 1971) are frameworks to name, not evidence to quote.

These are reasons for the design. They are not results from Ajimi. No participant testing has been done.

## Why learners stop

- No credible retention figure for a Japanese app was found.
- A McGill thesis on 79 Duolingo users lists reduced motivation, content that feels inauthentic, weak self-regulation and lack of guidance. https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/k643b581s

## What exists already

- Duolingo: themed lessons mixing vocabulary and grammar. WaniKani: radicals, then kanji, then vocabulary, with mnemonics and spaced repetition. Bunpro: grammar library. Renshuu: textbook-aligned quizzes, spaced repetition and a mascot, Kao-chan.
- No app that teaches Japanese through cooking turned up in a shallow search. Say "none found", not "none exists". A mascot is not unique to Ajimi.

## Sanuki udon

- **Verified.** Sanuki is the Edo-period name for Kagawa. The broth is dashi from small dried fish (iriko). Kake udon is noodles in a light clear broth with chopped spring onion. https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/H30-01110.html
- Unverified on an official page: the boil, rinse, rewarm sequence.
